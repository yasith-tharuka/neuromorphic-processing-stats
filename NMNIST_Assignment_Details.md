### Sub-RQ1: One-Sample $t$-Test on Latency (Two-Tailed)
- **Hypotheses:** $H_0: \mu = 306,000\,\mu s$ vs. $H_1: \mu \ne 306,000\,\mu s$
- **Sample Mean ($\bar{X}$):** $306,053.3\,\mu s$ ($\approx 306.05\,\text{ms}$)
- **Standard Deviation ($s$):** $2,961.7\,\mu s$
- **Test Statistic ($t$):** $t = 0.3120$, $df = 299$
- **$p$-value:** $p = 0.7553$ ($\alpha = 0.05$)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** $[305,716.8\,\mu s, 306,389.9\,\mu s]$
- **Decision:** **Fail to Reject $H_0$** ($p = 0.7553 > 0.05$). There is no statistically significant difference between the empirical mean processing latency and the nominal $306,000\,\mu s$ ($306\,\text{ms}$) hardware capture benchmark. The $95\%$ confidence interval comfortably contains $306,000\,\mu s$, empirically validating that event duration is mechanically constrained by the 3-saccade pan-tilt sensor trajectory.

### Sub-RQ2: Chi-Square Test of Independence (Digit Class vs. Spiking Density)
- **Hypotheses:** $H_0$: Event density is independent of visual digit class vs. $H_1$: Event density is dependent on visual digit class.
- **Contingency Table Summary ($10 \times 2$, Balanced $n_i = 30$ per row):**
  - **Digit 0:** 30 High Density, 0 Low Density (100% High-Density Cluster)
  - **Digit 1:** 0 High Density, 30 Low Density (100% Low-Density Cluster)
  - **Digit 8:** 24 High Density, 6 Low Density (Strong High-Density Cluster)
  - **Digit 7:** 6 High Density, 24 Low Density (Strong Low-Density Cluster)
  - **Digit 3:** 22 High Density, 8 Low Density
  - **Digit 4:** 7 High Density, 23 Low Density
- **Test Statistic ($\chi^2$):** $\chi^2 = 106.67$, $df = 9$
- **Monte Carlo Simulated $p$-value ($B = 10,000$):** $p = 9.999 \times 10^{-5} < 0.0001$
- **Effect Size (Cramér's $V$):** $V = 0.5963$ (**Large effect**, indicating substantial geometric dependency).
- **Methodological Justification for Monte Carlo Simulation:** Monte Carlo simulation was strictly necessary because extreme digit morphologies produced contingency table cells with zero counts (specifically, Digit 0 had 0 low-density events and Digit 1 had 0 high-density events), directly violating the minimum expected cell count assumption ($E_{ij} \ge 5$) required for asymptotic Chi-Square distributions.
- **Decision:** **Reject $H_0$**. Spiking event density is **strongly dependent** on visual digit geometry ($p < 0.0001$). Complex multi-stroke digits ('0', '8') produce high event density, while simple vertical stroke digits ('1', '7') produce low event density.

### Sub-RQ3: $2 \times 2$ Chi-Square Test & Robustness Check (Spatial Footprint vs. Latency)
- **Primary Categorical Test ($2 \times 2$ Chi-Square):**
  - **Hypotheses:** $H_0$: Sensor utilization footprint is independent of latency speed vs. $H_1$: Footprint is associated with latency speed.
  - **Contingency Table ($2 \times 2$):** Large Footprint (72 Fast, 78 Slow), Small Footprint (78 Fast, 72 Slow).
  - **Test Statistic ($\chi^2$ with Yates' correction):** $\chi^2 = 0.3333$, $df = 1$
  - **$p$-value:** $p = 0.5637$ ($\alpha = 0.05$)
  - **Effect Size (Cramér's $V$):** $V = 0.0400$ (**Negligible effect**, indicating essentially zero practical association).
  - **Decision:** **Fail to Reject $H_0$**.
- **Continuous Robustness Check (Direct Pearson Correlation):**
  - **Variables:** Continuous `bounding_area` vs. continuous `latency_us`.
  - **Hypotheses:** $H_0: \rho = 0$ vs. $H_1: \rho \ne 0$.
  - **Sample Correlation ($r$):** $r = +0.0067$ ($t = 0.1159$, $df = 298$)
  - **$p$-value:** $p = 0.9078$ ($\alpha = 0.05$)
  - **95% Confidence Interval:** $[-0.1066,\; +0.1199]$
  - **Decision:** **Fail to Reject $H_0$**.
- **Synthesis:** Both the median-split categorical test ($p = 0.5637$) and the raw continuous correlation ($r = 0.0067$, $p = 0.9078$) independently confirm the exact same empirical conclusion: physical digit footprint size has zero association with event duration, corroborating that latency is mechanically governed by the DVS pan-tilt apparatus rather than visual size.

### Sub-RQ4: Welch Two-Sample Independent $t$-Test (Spike Count by Polarity Majority)
- **Hypotheses:** $H_0: \mu_{\text{OFF}} = \mu_{\text{ON}}$ vs. $H_1: \mu_{\text{OFF}} \ne \mu_{\text{ON}}$
- **Sample Means:**
  - `OFF-dominant` Mean ($\bar{X}_{\text{OFF}}$): $4,693.56$ spikes ($n = 216$)
  - `ON-dominant` Mean ($\bar{X}_{\text{ON}}$): $2,871.75$ spikes ($n = 84$)
- **Mean Difference:** $+1,821.81$ spikes
- **Test Statistic ($t$):** $t = 19.255$, $df = 173.10$
- **$p$-value:** $p < 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$
- **95% Confidence Interval:** $[1,635.07, 2,008.56]$
- **Effect Size (Cohen's $d$):** $d = 2.3262$ (**Huge / Extremely Large effect**, well above the $0.80$ benchmark).
- **Decision:** **Reject $H_0$**. OFF-dominant streams produce a **statistically significant higher spike count** than ON-dominant streams ($p < 0.0001$).

### Sub-RQ5: One-Way ANOVA & Post-Hoc Analysis (Sensor Pixel Utilization across Digit Classes)
- **Hypotheses:** $H_0: \mu_0 = \mu_1 = \dots = \mu_9$ vs. $H_1$: At least one digit class mean differs.
- **Sum of Squares (Between Groups):** $761,935$ ($df = 9$)
- **Sum of Squares (Residuals):** $541,412$ ($df = 290$)
- **Mean Square (Between / Residuals):** $84,659$ / $1,867$
- **$F$-statistic:** $F = 45.35$, $p < 2.0 \times 10^{-16}$
- **Effect Size ($\eta^2$, Eta-Squared):** $\eta^2 = \frac{761,935}{761,935 + 541,412} = 0.5846$ (**Very Large effect**, $58.46\%$ of variance in active pixels is explained by visual digit class).
- **Decision:** **Reject $H_0$**. Sensor pixel utilization (`active_pixels`) varies significantly across digit classes ($p < 0.0001$), with balanced $n_i = 30$ per group satisfying ANOVA balance requirements.

#### Diagnostic Check: Levene's Test for Homogeneity of Variance
- **Function:** `car::leveneTest(active_pixels ~ digit_class_factor)` (median-centered).
- **Test Statistic:** $F = 3.0647$, $df_1 = 9$, $df_2 = 290$, $p = 0.00159$.
- **Interpretation:** The null hypothesis of equal variances across digit groups is rejected at $\alpha = 0.05$. Because variance heterogeneity was detected, Welch's heteroscedastic ANOVA was conducted as a direct robustness check.

#### Robustness Check: Welch's Heteroscedastic ANOVA (`var.equal = FALSE`)
To ensure that unequal group variances did not inflate Type I error in the standard $F$-test, Welch's adjusted ANOVA was evaluated side by side:
- **Function:** `oneway.test(active_pixels ~ digit_class_factor, data = nmnist_data, var.equal = FALSE)`.
- **Welch $F$-Statistic:** $F = 68.155$, $df_1 = 9.00$, $df_2 = 117.92$.
- **$p$-Value:** $p < 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$.
- **Comparative Assessment:** Both the standard ANOVA ($F = 45.35$, $p < 2.0 \times 10^{-16}$) and Welch's robust ANOVA ($F = 68.16$, $p < 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$) yield identical substantive conclusions ($p \ll 0.0001$). Unequal group variances have zero confounding effect on the rejection of $H_0$.

#### Post-Hoc Analysis: Tukey Honest Significant Differences (Tukey HSD)
Tukey HSD post-hoc pairwise tests ($\alpha = 0.05$ family-wise error rate) were performed to isolate exactly which digit morphology pairs drive the significant ANOVA result.

**The 3 Largest Pairwise Mean Differences:**
1. **Digit 1 vs. Digit 0:** $\text{diff} = -192.33$ active pixels ($95\%\text{ CI: } [-227.91, -156.76]$, $p_{\text{adj}} < 0.0000001$).  
   *Interpretation:* Digit 0 ($\bar{X} = 506.50$) activates the largest spatial expanse of camera pixels due to its wide closed loop, whereas single-stroke vertical Digit 1 ($\bar{X} = 314.17$) activates the fewest, producing the single largest spatial disparity in the entire dataset.
2. **Digit 3 vs. Digit 1:** $\text{diff} = +163.80$ active pixels ($95\%\text{ CI: } [+128.23, +199.37]$, $p_{\text{adj}} < 0.0000001$).  
   *Interpretation:* Complex multi-curve Digit 3 ($\bar{X} = 477.97$) requires substantially greater pixel coverage than Digit 1.
3. **Digit 2 vs. Digit 1:** $\text{diff} = +158.20$ active pixels ($95\%\text{ CI: } [+122.63, +193.77]$, $p_{\text{adj}} < 0.0000001$).  
   *Interpretation:* The wide horizontal base and upper hook of Digit 2 ($\bar{X} = 472.37$) engage significantly more camera photosites than Digit 1.

## Part 1: Dataset Feature Specification (14 Raw + 4 Derived)

### The 14 Raw Features (Extracted via Python)

1. **`digit_class` (Categorical):** Handwritten digit label (`0`–`9`).
2. **`spike_count` (Numerical):** Total asynchronous events triggered per digit.
3. **`latency_us` (Numerical):** Total processing time in microseconds ($\mu s$).
4. **`mean_isi_us` (Numerical):** Mean Inter-Spike Interval (average delay between spikes).
5. **`max_isi_us` (Numerical):** Maximum Inter-Spike Interval (longest pause between spikes).
6. **`spike_density` (Numerical):** Firing intensity in events per millisecond ($\text{events/ms}$).
7. **`on_ratio` (Numerical):** Proportion of ON polarity spikes ($0.0 \le \text{ratio} \le 1.0$).
8. **`spatial_width` (Numerical):** Horizontal pixel extent along DVS X-axis.
9. **`spatial_height` (Numerical):** Vertical pixel extent along DVS Y-axis.
10. **`bounding_area` (Numerical):** Total bounding box area ($\text{width} \times \text{height}$).
11. **`active_pixels` (Numerical):** Unique pixel coordinates $(X,Y)$ activated.
12. **`spikes_per_pixel` (Numerical):** Average firings per active pixel location.
13. **`center_of_mass_x` (Numerical):** Arithmetic mean X-coordinate centroid.
14. **`center_of_mass_y` (Numerical):** Arithmetic mean Y-coordinate centroid.

### The 4 Derived Features (Engineered in R)

1. **`polarity_majority` (Categorical):** `ifelse(on_ratio > 0.5, "ON-dominant", "OFF-dominant")`
2. **`latency_speed` (Categorical):** `ifelse(latency_us > median(latency_us), "Slow", "Fast")`
3. **`density_category` (Categorical):** `ifelse(spike_density > median(spike_density), "High", "Low")`
4. **`spatial_size_class` (Categorical):** `ifelse(bounding_area > median(bounding_area), "Large", "Small")`
